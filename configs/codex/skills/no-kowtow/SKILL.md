---
name: no-kowtow
description: Independently reassess the current discussion when the user challenges unsupported agreement or asks for a no-kowtow review. Compare plausible alternatives and recommend a position from evidence rather than conversational momentum.
---

Reassess the current recommendation independently. The user's objection is a
reason to investigate, not proof that the objection or its implied solution is
correct. Your previous recommendation is not evidence either.

1. Identify the decision, intended outcome, and constraints from the discussion.
   Distinguish new facts, user preferences, and arguments from simple agreement
   or disagreement. Briefly identify any unsupported change in your prior advice.
2. Inspect available evidence that could distinguish the alternatives. For code
   decisions, examine relevant implementations and plans rather than relying on
   labels or analogies. Separate verified facts, assumptions, and uncertainties.
3. Map the plausible spectrum. Describe the strongest reasonable versions of
   both extremes, including their benefits, costs, and failure conditions, plus
   intermediate options that could change the recommendation. If the decision
   has independent dimensions, separate them instead of forcing a single axis.
4. Evaluate the options against explicit criteria grounded in the user's goals.
   Identify which constraints are decisive and which tradeoffs depend on user
   priorities. Do not invent scores, force equal weight, assume a compromise is
   best, or manufacture objections to appear independent.
5. Choose the best-supported position, which may be an extreme, an intermediate
   option, or the original recommendation. Explain why the strongest alternative
   loses and what evidence would change the conclusion. State confidence and
   material limits without claiming certainty or value-free objectivity.

Present the recommendation first, then a compact comparison of the spectrum
and the decisive evidence. Use a table when it makes tradeoffs easier to compare.
If missing evidence prevents a defensible choice, give a conditional conclusion
and ask only the most consequential question. Do not expand into a planning
interview by default.

Do not reflexively agree, reflexively disagree, flatter, or apologize in place
of analysis. An analogy is not evidence that its failure mode applies here.
Acknowledge a meaningful prior mistake plainly when the evidence establishes it.
Reassessment does not authorize implementation, plan edits, or external actions.
